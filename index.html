Research

I build hybrid solvers that combine classical numerical methods with machine learning.

A question keeps coming up in my work: when a learned model sits inside a numerical solver, whether as a subdomain in domain decomposition or as the constitutive law at every integration point of a multiscale simulation, sometimes it is not enough to just guarantee the accuracy of its values; its derivatives matter as well.

I'm interested in making such solvers reliable, scalable and differentiable, with applications to composite materials.

Projects

  • Coupling Finite Elements and Neural Operators via Domain Decomposition
    Ongoing, unpublished  |  Johns Hopkins University  |  Apr 2026 – present

    A GPU-accelerated JAX solver that couples finite element and neural operator (Transolver) subdomains through non-overlapping Neumann–Dirichlet domain decomposition, with constant, Aitken and IQN-ILS (Anderson-type) interface accelerators, scaled from a 2D cylinder to a 3D fiber composite with 100 learned subdomains and a J2-plastic bolted joint under cyclic loading.

  • Multiscale Modeling of Fiber Composites with Neural Operator RVE Surrogates
    Ongoing, unpublished  |  collaborating with Prof. Satyajit Mojumder (WSU)  |  Aug 2026 – present

    Replacing the RVE solve at every macroscale integration point of FE² with a neural operator that takes realistic microstructures (e.g. fiber clustering and resin-rich regions) and returns homogenized stress, with consistent tangents from automatic differentiation inside a differentiable JAX finite element solver.

  • Enhancing Deep Finite Element Method (DFEM) for Heterogeneous Heat Transfer via Exact Boundary Constraints and Gradient Supervision
    Yongmeng Wu
    Manuscript in revision  |  oral presentation in English at ‘101 Project’ Undergraduate Forum in Mechanics, 2026

    Hard Dirichlet constraints via distance functions, Fourier features and gradient supervision at material interfaces give relative L2 errors below 0.1% on 2D benchmarks and about 0.3% on a 3D woven composite; parametric warm-starting is about 10× faster than conventional FEM.
